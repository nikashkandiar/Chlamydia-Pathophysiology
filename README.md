CHLAMYDIA: Pathophysiology & Reproductive Health Editorial
Author: Nikash Parasuraman  
Module: HBS3001 — Communicating Disease  
Target Audience: Sexually active young women aged 15–25

---
📖 Overview & Intended Setting
This interactive digital magazine article ("CHLAMYDIA: Pathophysiology & Reproductive Health Editorial") is designed to be accessed on a tablet, smartphone, or desktop computer in a:
University student health clinic
General practitioner (GP) or sexual health clinic waiting room (via a QR code poster)
Online youth health portal
It is written so a young woman aged 15 to 25 with no prior university-level biology background can independently explore the scientific processes underlying Chlamydia trachomatis at her own pace in 5 to 8 minutes.
---
🧭 How to Use and Navigate the Interactive Website
1. Opening the Website
Click the website link in any standard web browser (Chrome, Safari, or Edge). No software installation or account login is required.
---
2. Navigating Between Sections (Left Sidebar Directory Box)
Use the sticky "Article Sections" navigation box on the left side of the screen at any time to jump directly between the 5 chapters:
- 01. Overview — Epidemiology in Singapore and why ages 15 to 25 are vulnerable
- 02. Healthy Physiology — Normal function of the reproductive tract
- 03. Pathophysiology — The 4-stage biphasic cellular life cycle
- 04. Clinical Link — How cellular disruption causes symptoms and complications
- 05. Knowledge Check — 4-question interactive review quiz
---
3. Reading Highlighted Concepts & Glossary Pills
Throughout the article, key scientific processes are highlighted in colour-coded pastel markers:
Marker Colour	Meaning
🟢 Green	Healthy Physiology
🟣 Purple	Bacterial Mechanism
🔴 Red	Immune Response & Pathology
🟡 Yellow	Simplified Note
Interactive Glossary: Clicking any purple term pill in the text opens the slide-out Scientific Glossary drawer on the right, which provides both a plain-language overview and a detailed cellular definition.
---
4. Exploring Chapter 02 (Healthy Physiology Interactive Simulators)
Click the three tabs at the top of the Chapter 02 interactive box:
The Cervical Epithelium & Mucus Barrier
Fallopian Tube Physiology
Baseline Mucosal Immunity
Inside Tab 1: Click the "Immature Cervix (15–25)" vs. "Mature Cervix (Adult)" toggle buttons to compare how exposed simple columnar cells make young women more susceptible to bacterial entry.
Inside Tab 2: Click the "Healthy Coordinated Cilia Wave" vs. "Post-Inflammatory Deciliated & Scarred Tube" toggle buttons to watch an animated simulation of how ciliated cells and secretory peg cells normally transport and nourish the egg (ovum), and how scarring blocks this pathway.
---
5. Exploring Chapters 03 & 04 (Interactive 3D Flip-Flashcards & Dual-Exit Simulator)
In Chapter 03: Click through Stage 1 to Stage 4 (or use the Previous / Next Stage buttons) to follow Chlamydia trachomatis as it enters, replicates inside, and exits a host cell. In Stage 4, toggle between Outcome 1: Extrusion and Outcome 2: Host Cell Lysis below the diagram.
Interactive Two-Sided Flashcards (Chapters 03 & 04): In both chapters, the main explanation is presented on an interactive 3D flashcard:
Side A (Front — Default): Displays the Simplified Translation in clear, everyday language.
Side B (Back — On Click): Click anywhere on the flashcard (or click the "Flip to Cellular Mechanism" button) to flip the card in 3D and read the detailed Cellular & Molecular Mechanism on the back. Click the card again to flip back.
---
6. Completing Chapter 05 (Knowledge Check Quiz)
Scroll to the bottom of the page, select one answer for each of the 4 multiple-choice questions, and click "Check My Answers" to receive instant feedback and explanations. Click "Reset Quiz" to try again.
